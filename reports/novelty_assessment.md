# Novelty assessment

## Search scope

A targeted search was completed on 2026-09-01 after confirming that the repository contained no usable reference list. The search covered Dhaka heatwave climatology, Bangladesh definitions, persistent and compound events, temperature trends, antecedent meteorological conditions, count regression, and forecast validation. Peer-reviewed publisher records and DOI-bearing journal pages were prioritized. The detailed extraction is in `reports/literature_gap_matrix.csv`.

## What prior work already establishes

The article must not claim that heatwaves, temperature trends, nighttime heat, or meteorological precursors have not been studied in Bangladesh. Nissan et al. (2017) developed a health-linked compound day/night definition and examined predictability and dry antecedent conditions. Khatun et al. (2017) quantified temperature trends in Dhaka Division. Rashid et al. (2024) and Mallik et al. (2024) described Bangladesh threshold-day climatology and spatially varying trends. Islam et al. (2024) studied Dhaka heatwave/LST patterns and surveyed exposed people. Tabassum et al. (2024) directly analyzed Dhaka urban–rural temperature contrasts, percentile heatwaves, circulation, and UHI–heatwave interaction. Molla et al. (2025) compared observation- and ERA5-based definitions, while Chaki et al. (2025) validated WRF thermal-stress predictions for selected events. Farukh et al. (2026) extended national pre-monsoon heatwave and circulation analysis through 2024.

These studies mean the new article cannot defensibly use “first,” “no previous study,” or “first comprehensive analysis.” It also cannot present an urban-heat-island conclusion without reproducing the urban–rural design used by Tabassum et al.

## Defensible analytical gap

Within the verified closely related literature, the common emphasis is descriptive frequency, spatial patterns, severity categories, percentile selection, health-linked definition development, UHI interaction, reanalysis comparison, circulation, or selected-event forecasting. The targeted search did not identify a Dhaka article that combines all of the following within one long daily record:

1. an explicit primary persistent absolute-threshold outcome;
2. systematic comparison of one-day, two-day, three-day, calendar-day percentile, and compound day/night definitions;
3. formal HAC temperature trends with a direct \(T_{\min}-T_{\max}\) slope contrast;
4. Poisson-versus-NB2 comparison for annual persistent heatwave-day counts;
5. distribution-consistent count diagnostics and influential-year sensitivity;
6. leakage-safe antecedent features created with a one-day shift;
7. a repeated-measures logistic GEE with event-onset and lag-window sensitivity; and
8. pooled chronological out-of-sample validation against a seasonal-trend base model.

The contribution is this integrated inferential design and the scientific distinction it reveals, not a claim that the topic or any component method is unprecedented.

## Recommended novelty wording

Use:

> Few Dhaka-focused studies have combined alternative event definitions, direct count regression, adjusted antecedent meteorological associations, and chronological model validation within one long daily series.

Also acceptable:

> This study extends Dhaka heatwave climatology by testing whether conclusions remain stable across event definitions and by directly modeling overdispersed persistent heatwave-day counts with diagnostic and temporal-validation safeguards.

Do not use:

- “This is the first study.”
- “No previous study has examined Dhaka heatwaves.”
- “The first comprehensive analysis.”
- “The study establishes meteorological drivers.”
- “The study demonstrates urban amplification.”

## Originality verdict

The paper has **methodological-combination and inference-level originality**, not topic-level priority. Its most publishable result is the contrast between clear background warming and statistically uncertain primary persistent-count change, together with the finding that compound day/night trends differ from the primary absolute-persistence result. The strong antecedent associations add value because they are leakage-safe, clustered, sensitivity-tested, and chronologically validated, but they remain observational.

## Residual manual checks

Before submission, the corresponding author should import all cited records into a reference manager, verify author order and pagination against the final publisher PDFs, check whether the 2025 Climate Services record received corrected bibliographic metadata, and update the search immediately before submission. A domain expert should also decide whether the 2026 literature is appropriate for a manuscript framed around data ending in 2024.
