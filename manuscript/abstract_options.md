# Abstract options

## Option 1 — recommended structured abstract

**Background.** Long-term warming does not guarantee that heatwave counts increase uniformly, because event estimates depend on thresholds, persistence requirements, and day/night criteria. We evaluated these distinctions in a daily Dhaka meteorological series spanning 1972–2024.

**Methods.** Complete-calendar-year temperature trends through 2023 and complete March–June trends through 2024 were estimated using ordinary least squares with heteroskedasticity-and-autocorrelation-consistent standard errors, Theil–Sen slopes, and Mann–Kendall tests. Six heatwave definitions were compared. The primary outcome was the annual March–June count of days in events with \(T_{\max}\geq36\,{}^\circ\mathrm{C}\) for at least three consecutive days. Poisson and NB2 count models were compared. Leakage-safe lagged predictors were evaluated with logistic generalized estimating equations and chronological validation.

**Results.** Annual mean \(T_{\max}\) increased by 0.192 °C per decade (95% CI 0.130–0.253), and March–June mean \(T_{\max}\) increased by 0.165 °C per decade (95% CI 0.044–0.287). The primary count distribution was strongly overdispersed (variance/mean 11.56), favoring NB2 over Poisson (AIC 245.2 versus 659.8). The primary persistent heatwave-day trend was uncertain (IRR 1.029 per decade, 95% CI 0.727–1.455; \(p=0.872\)). A secondary compound day/night definition showed a nominal positive trend (IRR 1.320, 95% CI 1.043–1.672; \(p=0.0209\)), but not after adjustment across definitions (\(q=0.126\)). Lower antecedent humidity (OR 0.307), lower precipitation (OR 0.140), and higher wind speed (OR 1.337) were associated with persistent days; directions remained stable for event onset. Pooled out-of-sample PR-AUC improved from 0.198 for seasonality and trend alone to 0.473 for the antecedent model, although calibration showed underprediction.

**Conclusions.** Dhaka warmed detectably, but heatwave-count conclusions were definition-dependent and the primary persistent-event rate showed no clear monotonic change. Antecedent associations were predictive rather than causal. Forecast validation did not justify exact five-year heatwave counts.

## Option 2 — concise unstructured abstract

We analyzed 19,316 consecutive daily meteorological records for Dhaka from 1 January 1972 through 18 November 2024 to distinguish background warming from changes in heatwave occurrence. Annual temperature trends used complete years through 2023; March–June 2024 was retained because all 122 dates were present. We compared six heatwave definitions, directly modeled annual March–June persistent heatwave-day counts, and evaluated adjusted antecedent meteorological associations using leakage-safe lag construction, year-clustered logistic generalized estimating equations, and chronological validation. Annual mean maximum temperature increased by 0.192 °C per decade (95% CI 0.130–0.253), while March–June mean maximum temperature increased by 0.165 °C per decade (95% CI 0.044–0.287). Persistent counts were zero in 31 of 53 seasons and strongly overdispersed, so NB2 was selected over Poisson. The primary trend estimate was IRR 1.029 per decade (95% CI 0.727–1.455; \(p=0.872\)), providing no evidence of monotonic change. Trend conclusions differed by definition: the combined high-maximum/high-minimum definition had an IRR of 1.320 (95% CI 1.043–1.672). Lower prior-three-day humidity, lower prior-seven-day precipitation, and higher prior-three-day wind speed distinguished persistent heatwave days after seasonal and long-term adjustment; pooled out-of-sample validation improved over a seasonal-trend base model. Dhaka therefore experienced detectable warming without a correspondingly clear trend in the prespecified persistent heatwave-day rate. Forecasting was retained only as supplementary validation because complex models did not outperform a climatology-plus-trend baseline and exact future event counts were not supported.

## Option 3 — methods-forward abstract

Heatwave climatology can be distorted when descriptive threshold counts are interpreted without testing persistence, reference climatology, count dispersion, or temporal dependence. Using a continuous Dhaka daily record for 1972–2024, we prespecified persistent heat as \(T_{\max}\geq36\,{}^\circ\mathrm{C}\) for at least three days and compared it with five alternative definitions. HAC temperature trends, Poisson and NB2 count regression, NB2-consistent diagnostics, logistic GEE, onset and lag-window sensitivity, and strictly chronological validation were applied. Warming was evident in annual maximum and minimum temperatures, but their annual slopes did not differ (difference 0.016 °C per decade, 95% CI -0.034–0.067). The primary annual hot-season count had mean 5.09, variance 58.89, and 58.5% zero seasons. NB2 fit substantially better than Poisson, yet its decadal IRR was 1.029 (95% CI 0.727–1.455; \(p=0.872\)). The compound percentile day/night definition showed a nominal positive trend, but it did not survive multiplicity adjustment across definitions. Humidity, precipitation, and wind associations survived event-onset and influential-season checks, and the full antecedent GEE achieved pooled out-of-sample ROC-AUC 0.924, PR-AUC 0.473, Brier score 0.033, and calibration slope 1.136; the calibration intercept of 1.512 indicated underprediction. These findings demonstrate that clear warming and uncertain persistent-event count trends can coexist. They also show why correlations and model complexity alone are insufficient evidence for independent meteorological associations or reliable long-horizon forecasts.
