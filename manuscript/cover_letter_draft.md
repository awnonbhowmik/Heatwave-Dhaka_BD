# Draft cover letter

Dear Editor,

Please consider our provisional manuscript, **“Long-Term Warming and Definition-Dependent Heatwaves in Dhaka, Bangladesh: Count Trends and Antecedent Meteorological Associations, 1972–2024,”** for consideration as an Original Article in *Meteorology and Atmospheric Physics*.

The manuscript addresses a practical methodological problem in heatwave climatology: a clear long-term warming signal does not imply that every event definition has the same trend. Using 19,316 consecutive daily observations, we compare absolute, persistence-based, calendar-day percentile, and compound daytime–nighttime definitions. We prespecify March–June days within events having \(T_{\max}\geq36\,{}^\circ\mathrm{C}\) for at least three consecutive days as the primary outcome, directly model its overdispersed annual counts using NB2 regression, and report diagnostics and influential-year sensitivity.

The principal result is deliberately modest. Annual and hot-season temperatures warmed, but the primary persistent heatwave-day rate did not show a detectable monotonic trend. A compound day/night definition produced a nominal positive trend that did not survive adjustment across six definition tests, demonstrating why definitions and multiplicity must be explicit. A secondary logistic generalized-estimating-equation analysis evaluates strictly antecedent meteorological conditions, excludes target-derived variables, and is supported by event-onset sensitivity and chronological validation. Forecast validation is supplementary because a simple climatology-plus-trend model outperformed SARIMAX and exact five-year event counts were not defensible.

The contribution is an integrated, reproducible inferential analysis rather than a claim of topic-level priority. The paper does not infer human vulnerability, urban-heat-island change, tree-cover causation, or health effects. Source hashes, configuration, tests, generated tables and figures, diagnostics, and a claim-to-evidence audit accompany the analysis.
